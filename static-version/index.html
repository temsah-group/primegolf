Ask a Question
Do I need to visit Dubai?

Yes — if you do not yet hold UAE residency, a visit is required to complete certain formalities such as biometrics and ID issuance. We will guide you through the process to make it as smooth as possible.

How long does the visa process take?

The visa process typically takes 5–7 working days, depending on the specific case and authority approvals.

Can I keep my EU company?

Yes — you can absolutely keep your existing EU company while setting up your UAE structure. Many of our clients operate internationally and benefit from having both entities in place.

Can I open a company without being in Dubai?

Yes, it is possible to establish a company remotely, even if you have never visited Dubai before. However, if you would like to obtain a residency visa or Emirates ID, you will eventually need to travel to the UAE. Many people either enter on a tourist visa and complete a status change locally, or apply for an entry visa beforehand, which is often more cost-effective. Once an entry visa has been issued, you usually need to enter the UAE within 60 days.

Do I need residency in Dubai to set up a company?

UAE residency is not mandatory to set up a company in Dubai. However, depending on your business structure and long-term plans, obtaining residency can offer several advantages.

What is an FZE / FZC / Free Zone company?

An FZE or FZC is a company established within one of Dubai’s free zones. They are popular among international entrepreneurs for their simplified setup and significant tax advantages. Owners typically receive a three-year residence visa, while employee visas are generally issued for two years and can usually be renewed as long as the company remains active. The company license must be renewed annually; depending on the free zone and license category, yearly renewal costs are often around €5,000 or more.

How expensive is life in Dubai?

Overall, the cost of living in Dubai is fairly comparable to many parts of Europe. Fresh produce, fuel, car insurance, private healthcare, services and cars are often more affordable, while rent, mobile plans, clothing and internet can be higher depending on your lifestyle and area. Cities such as London, Zurich, Paris, Amsterdam or Munich frequently rank higher in cost-of-living comparisons.

Which language is spoken in Dubai?

Dubai is extremely international — Emiratis make up less than 10% of the population. English is widely spoken and is the main language of business. Arabic is the official language, and road signs, contracts and government documents are generally in both Arabic and English.

What is life like for women in Dubai?

Dubai is generally considered very safe and comfortable for women, with no restrictions on driving, working or clothing choices and no requirement to wear a veil in public. Certain government buildings may require modest clothing, such as long trousers — a rule that also applies to men.

How expensive is renting property in Dubai?

Rental prices vary by area but are generally comparable to major German cities, and properties are often much more spacious. Rent was traditionally paid a year in advance, though many landlords now accept quarterly or monthly payments. Utilities are handled through DEWA, and some listings are “Chiller Free”. Popular platforms include Dubizzle and Property Finder.

Do you pay taxes in Dubai?

There is no personal income tax, and businesses often benefit from very low taxation. VAT is only 5%. To fully benefit, many people relocate their tax residency to the UAE and reduce time spent in countries such as Germany — the commonly referenced “183-day rule”. Depending on your circumstances, there may also be legal optimization strategies that don’t require full relocation.

Isn’t Dubai too hot?

Dubai averages around 28°C year-round with very little rain. Summers (June–September) can exceed 40°C, so many residents spend more time outdoors in the evenings. Winters are especially pleasant at around 24°C, with evenings rarely below 15°C, and the sea is warm enough for swimming almost every day.
